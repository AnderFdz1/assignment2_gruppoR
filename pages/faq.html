THE BASICS

About Funds TOgether

How does the programme work from start to finish?

The programme has four phases. First, research teams take part in practical training on crowdfunding. Second, teams submit an application and the University selects up to five projects. Third, Ginger campaign managers help the selected teams design, launch and promote their campaigns. Fourth, when a campaign reaches 100% of its goal, the University doubles the funds raised, up to €10,000 per project. Training comes before the application: applying is not the same as launching a campaign immediately.

Is crowdfunding a replacement for research grants?

No. Crowdfunding is intended to complement traditional research funding, not replace it. It diversifies funding sources, brings research closer to society and helps teams build skills in project management, fundraising and communication.

MAKING A CONTRIBUTION

Supporting a project

How do I support a project?

Open a project from Explore Projects and select “Support this project”. Choose one of the suggested amounts (€10, €25, €50 or €100) or enter a custom amount of at least €5, then complete the form, choose a payment method and confirm. After confirming, you will see a confirmation page with the amount of your contribution.

Where does my contribution go?

Your contribution is linked to the specific project you choose, not to a general Funds TOgether fund. Each project page shows its own funding goal, progress and, where available, a breakdown of how the funds are planned to be used.

Can I contribute anonymously?

Yes. Select “Make my contribution anonymous” and your first name and surname will not be shown in the public supporters list. An email address is still required to complete the contribution and, on a live campaign, to receive confirmation.

Which payment methods can I use?

The checkout offers credit or debit card and PayPal.

Is my contribution an investment?

No. A contribution is a donation to a research project. It does not give you ownership, equity, a financial return or a share of the project's results. The project page explains the research and how the team plans to use the funds.

Are contributions tax-deductible?

Tax treatment depends on the campaign and on your own situation; check the campaign terms or contact the campaign team before contributing.

Can I change or cancel a contribution?

Contact the campaign team as soon as possible if you need help with a contribution; they can explain what is possible before the campaign closes.

DURING AND AFTER A CAMPAIGN

Campaigns and results

What do “Active” and “Completed” mean?

An active campaign is still open and can accept contributions until the deadline shown on its project page. A completed campaign has closed: the page and its updates stay visible, but it no longer accepts new contributions and its funding total no longer changes.

What happens if a campaign does not reach 100% of its goal?

A campaign closes at the end of its campaign period whether or not it reaches its target. The University's co-funding is unlocked only when a campaign reaches 100% of its goal, so a campaign that ends below 100% does not receive the matching contribution. The campaign page shows the final result.

Why can a campaign show more than 100%?

Campaigns can raise more than their goal. The progress bar stops at 100% for display, but the percentage shows the real total, which may be 127% or more. This extra support is called overfunding and does not change the €10,000 cap on University co-funding.

How exactly does University co-funding work?

Co-funding is applied after a campaign reaches 100% of its goal. The University adds an amount equal to the funds raised, up to €10,000 per project. For example, a campaign with a €12,000 goal that raises €12,000 receives €10,000 in co-funding, not €12,000, because of the cap. A campaign that raises €6,000, or 50% of its goal, does not receive co-funding.

What do the success rate and average overfunding figures mean?

The success rate is the share of past Funds TOgether campaigns that reached their funding goal; the site reports 100% so far. Average overfunding is the average amount raised above the goal across previous editions, reported as 369%. These are historical results, not a guarantee that a future campaign will succeed or exceed its target.

How can I follow or share a campaign?

Every project page has a funding panel, News & Updates, Comments and Supporters sections, so you can follow progress without creating an account. Use the “Share this project” button to send the link through your device or copy it to your clipboard.

FOR RESEARCH TEAMS

Applying and co-funding

How do I apply to launch a campaign?

Use the “Launch a Campaign” button and complete the application form with your details, project description, funding goal and team information. This is an application, not an instant publication: the University reviews submissions and selects up to five projects for the edition.

What is the minimum funding goal, and what does it represent?

The minimum funding goal you can enter is €500. The goal is the amount the campaign aims to raise and the threshold used to decide whether it reaches 100% and unlocks University co-funding, so it should be realistic and carefully planned. Where available, the project page can show how the team plans to allocate that amount.

What support do selected teams receive?

Selected teams receive practical training, personalised guidance from Ginger campaign managers and help designing, implementing and promoting the campaign. The University co-funds the campaign only if it reaches 100% of its goal, up to €10,000 per project. Selection is competitive and does not guarantee that a campaign will reach its target.
